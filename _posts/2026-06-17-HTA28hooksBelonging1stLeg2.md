## Guiding Questions

* Chapter One___ What main themes does hooks introduce in this chapter to get the reader familiarized with the rest of her discussion in the text. Be specific.

* Chapter Two___ Where does hooks travel across her life and why does she return to her native Kentucky eventually?

* Chapter Three___ What role do mountains and hills play in identity formation; what is mountaintop removal; why is mountaintop removal motivated by profit; and how does it harm the rest of the ecology and the people who live there?

* Chapter Four___ How does city life, and especially the force migration of Black peoples from the agrarian south, estrange one from the land and from the earth? How does this estrangement further our alienation from not only the Divine but also other members of our community, including humans, animals, and land?

* Chapter Five___ What role does farming play in anti-racism and resistant to rural racism? Relate this to self-sustainability and to having higher powers than white supremacy. How also do Black farmers offer a more ecological, environmentally-friendly, and interdependent mode of farming and caregiving of the land? What might anti-racist modes of resistance teach us about anti-dominator modes of resisting ecological and environmental colonization by profiteering corporations?

* Chapter Six___ How does racism in hooks’s native Kentucky teach anti-racist structures? How does white supremacy reinscribe systems of hatred, domination, and hierarchy? Why are hierarchies always the issue at stake in decolonization? How does hooks describe the relationship between prejudice against race and prejudice against poverty? What similarities does she see between poor Black people and poor white people, or “hillbillies?” How does racism, prejudice, and anti-poverty hostility estrange our own selves from God? How does hooks notice different but similar prejudices the world over, even as she traveled to New York and California, and to urban city centers in general?

* Chapter Seven___ Explain the modes, histories, practices, and examples of segregation mentioned by hooks in Chapter Seven.
