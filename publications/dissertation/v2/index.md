# {{ page.title }}

## On Hermeneutical Materialism:
## A Material Critique of Communication, Selves, and Worldings in Benjamin, Lugones, and Dufrenne

#### {{ page.date | date_to_string: "ordinal", "US" }}

This dissertation explores two dimensions of the same thing. First, this dissertation articulates the linguistic and aesthetic dimensions of the co-constitution of multiple selves and multiple worlds. Second, from a different slant, it addresses the sociopolitical and aesthetic dimensions of “world” by pursuing dual-threads of questioning as they appear in the works of Mikel Dufrenne and of María Lugones, with critical, unifying insights from Walter Benjamin on material language. The “dual-questions” which this paper pursues are (a) how does the spectator travel from the objective world to the world of the aesthetic object, a la Dufrenne, and (b) how do we travel from our own lived world, a la Lugones, to the lived worlds of others in acts of empathy and of loving perception. This paper holds that these both are in fact the same question posed in different contexts. As such, I seek herein to find a unified conception of world that coherently allows for a critical aesthetics – a theory of art that allows for, is bolstered by, and intentionally promotes social progress. At the most basic level, I draw from Benjamin, first, the notion of aura to explain how a work of art can serve as a time capsule of a lived person and, second, the notion of a _weak_ messianic power of polyvocality and anti-canonical, open interpretation to argue that a spectator has the ceaseless capacity to let the dead speak, through feeling, from beyond the grave. However, by articulating the resolution of 'world'-traveling in both aesthetic contexts in Dufrenne and in sociopolotico-material contexts in Lugones, as well as by viewing Benjaminian communication and translation as forms of 'world'-traveling, we in fact articulate a critique of the productions of plural subjectivities and plural worlds as the necessary preconditions for these ideas to cohere, for any traveling to be possible. As such, mere articulations of world prove insufficient for  addressing the necessary preconditions. Rather, we must articulate a material critique of immanence itself. Overall, because this construction of immanence is grounded in hermeneutics, matter, and the polyvocal productions of subject-constructions, and because of Benjamin's own emphasis on material hermeneutics, that is, the diverse productions of unique perceptions of the same matter through the affective imagination, I refer to this body of thinking as hermeneutical materialism [HM]. HM places heavy emphases on history, affection, causation, redemption through hermeneutics and intellection, our limited possibilities for agency, subject-construction, polyvocality, and the indeterminacy of the real. My interest in articulating this theory lies in producing a robust theoretical and meta-ethical backbone for class conscious anarcho-communism.

For my dissertation, I propose to articulate a material critique of the productions of the constructions of subjectivities. In doing so, I hope to also articulate a material critique of the preconditions for the possibility of consciousness interfacing with and through its worlds. Thus, my work blends language, subjectivity, and worlds in a process that I refer to as worlding. I intend some ambiguity in worlding because I do not believe that the directionality nor the strict partitions of this world-making or world-listening process are readily evident from the outset of my inquiry. As such, by worlding I intend: a notion of sense-making, a notion of hermeneutical perception, and a notion of aretaic desert. I should like to say that the world is not only given to us but indeed also deserved or reflective of one's virtue or vice. Again, it is not clear if our world only *seems* to articulate our own desert, i.e., strictly at the level of hermeneutical perception, or whether this hermeneutical perception is active from the world itself, or even further whether the world actively changes itself in order to constitute a deeper reflection of one's aretaic desert, specifically related to ethical arete, i.e., arete of relations with others, choice-making, principles, work-ethic, accrued intuition or wisdom, previous sufferings, etc. To articulate this material critique of the relation between the subject(s) multiple constitution and worldings, I employ the writings and thinking of three thinkers: Walter Benjamin, María Lugones, and Mikel Dufrenne. In the following paragraph, I develop the material critique into a particular thesis statement or hypothesis. I state this thesis in multiple ways to better communicate to my reader the various aspects of the project I have in mind, which is richly comprehensive but is not contradictory nor outside the realm of material/immanent critique. In the immediately next paragraph after that, I explain why I call this project a material critique or an immanent critique rather than a transcendental critique in a strictly Kantian tradition.

I argue that multiple selves in one body, multiple overlapping worlds, and multiple selves in multiple other bodies all co-constitute each other unceasingly and porously at all times, without being able to be reduced to a single unity irrespective of historically- and socially-constructed differences. I argue this claim is proven by the necessary preconditions of the arguments of Benjamin, Lugones, and Dufrenne. For these thinkers works to be, shall we say, justified or epistemically insightful, prophetic even, then I argue the above thesis must be true. The thesis must be true, again, because the necessary metaphysico-ontological preconditions of the possibility of the sets of phenomenological evidence from the above thinkers implies such a thesis; thus this critique *is* transcendental in the mode of coming before the evidence given, but it is not to be considered transcendental as in a separate realm preexistent to the adumbration of material phenomena.

I say immanent here in the footsteps of Deleuze's "Immanence: A Life," especially when he says, "[a transcendental field] appears therefore as a pure stream of a-subjective consciousness, a pre-reflexive impersonal consciousness, a qualitative duration of consciousness without a self." Deleuze later says that immanence produces transcendence, and that immanence is immanent only to immanence itself rather than some supernumerary principle which would transcend preexisting boundaries. Again, this is why I am interested in articulating a difference-oriented process monism whose possibility depends on the multiple constructions of selves and of overlapping worlds with intermeshing oppressions. Finally, Deleuze states that we shall say immanence is "*a life*, and nothing else," *a life* which seeks self-preservation and operates materially as well as consciously. In fact consciousness and matter are not separate on this view nor under any of these thinkers. Thus, in materially articulating the preconditions for the possibility of the coherence of the writings of Benjamin, Lugones, and Dufrenne, I aim to add a new dimension to my work on A LIFE. I hope to continue this work with later sets of thinkers on language, communication, critical aesthetics, and metaphysics. Herein, I also employ material critique to emphasize the extended, social, relational, historical, and interactive elements constitutive of productions of behaviors, affective potentialities, and charged moments awaiting enaction.

Here I wish to say a bit more in-depth, if my reader will bear with me, about the core ideas I notice from across these three thinkers, as well as how I am able to construct my overall argument by weaving their works together. I do not intend to explain all their writings or key ideas in such a short proposal, but I have included the key notions essential to articulating a sketch of the structure of the selves-worlds multiplicity. Again, the list may come across as long for each thinker, but were I given adequate pages, such as in my dissertation, I intend to go more into depth of the writings and ideas. For now, I offer instead a roadmap of the structure of the dissertation and the aforementioned metaphysical and material convictions I propose.

When bringing these three thinkers together, I can construct an argument for the essential entanglement of my consciousness with the world and with my capacities for agency as co-constituted affectively with other selves in complex historical causation systems. Elements of these claims are not new, but what is new, I argue, is to approach this topic through material language, epistemology, and worlding. Moreover, I argue further new contributions by bringing together evidence for the co-constitution of multiple selves-worlds as necessary not only for perception of an 'outside' world and not only for consciousness to interface with a changing intentionality structure but also for a coherent metaphysics at all, which is the particular and final level I am most interested in realizing in this dissertation. The thesis specifically develops communion with matter as language, proto-linguistic thought, and eidetic-noetic justification alongside the construction of multiplicities of overlapping selves-worlds.

Here, I catalogue each thinker's key ideas. To argue for the inextricable connection between language, multiple selves, and polyvocal worlds, as well as that such inextricability becomes the necessary preconditions for the possibility of our experiencing art worlds, others' worlds, or the material world at all, I draw specifically on (i) Benjamin's writings on language, translation, historicity, art, architecture, aura, the lifework [worlding], reification [loss of aura, of historicity, and of world], mechanical reproduction, naming, and God. In a second section, I offer readings of  (ii) Lugones's *Pilgrimages/Peregrinajes* on ontological pluralism, coalitions, feminism, racism as willful or lazy ignorance of racialized systems of oppression [as a model for the structures of the dominators in all systems of intermeshing oppressions], interactive difference, 'worlds', transformation, cultural/epistemic imperialism, and forms of resisting oppressions which include: curdling-separation, traveling, trespassing, streetwalker theorizing, the limen, translation as non-equivalent creation, and Aristotle's practical syllogism as enacting semiotic symbols of intentionality and resistance [which are enactivistic statements of semiotic and behavioral language]. In a third section, I flesh out worlding further, as well as the inscription of the author or laborer into a work, through (iii) Dufrenne's writings on aesthetic phenomenology, the world of the aesthetic object, inscription, communication, the world of the creator, language, and the a priori. In a fourth section, I put all three thinkers in specific conversation with each other in smaller elemental fragments exemplary of the multidimensional constructions from these three writers.

Benjamin shows matter as both in communion with humans, as interpretive from the very beginning, and as constructive of sense-making. Architecture is a wonderful example because it is both aesthetic and functions so imposingly around us on all sides. Dufrenne allows us to conceive directly of the lifeworld of the author into the creation of the work. In this process, we produce time capsules of selves inscribing causal histories into the things we create. These subjectivities are passed along in further constructions of the selves in new experiences with new aesthetic objects, in new worlds of sense, new worlds of sense-making, new worlds of oneselves, and new worlds of/with others in politically resistant traveling. Here Lugones provides many resources, insofar as she explicitly discusses possibilities for political resistant under notions of multiple subjectivities cooperative within a body. This cooperativity of subjectivities within a body as well as that these cooperating multiplicities operate in all other selves further enriches the aesthetic dimension of *a life*, which is mentally gratifying to articulate and to animate -- i.e., to incant, to perceive, to invoke, to experience, etc. -- upon the perceptual reading of any given audience or self, in sharing affect and passing along new subjective modes of resistance across and with one another.
